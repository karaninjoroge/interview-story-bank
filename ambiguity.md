# Ambiguity Stories

---

## Story 1: Designing Without a Brief

**Question This Answers:** "Tell me about a time you had to work with incomplete information or significant ambiguity."
**Competency:** Dealing with ambiguity, structured thinking, initiative

---

**Situation:**
When I started the hospital queue management case study, there was no brief, no stakeholder, and no data. I had a problem domain — long queues in public hospitals — and nothing else. No user research, no constraints document, no engineering team to consult. I had to decide where to start with no guidance on what "done" looked like.

**Task:**
My task was to run a complete PM discovery and definition process from scratch, making decisions about scope, methodology, and direction entirely on my own.

**Action:**
The first thing I did was resist starting with a solution. My instinct was to start wireframing. Instead, I wrote out everything I thought I knew about the problem — and then marked each item as either "fact" or "assumption." Almost everything I thought I knew was an assumption.

I then chose which assumptions were most critical to validate — the ones where being wrong would most fundamentally change the product direction. I designed my research around those, not around confirming what I already believed.

When research came back with findings that contradicted my assumptions, I updated my direction without defending the original. That ability to change course based on evidence — not just gather evidence while staying on the original course — is what I consider the core skill for working in ambiguity.

**Result:**
The product that emerged was meaningfully different from what I would have built without research, and I can document exactly where and why it changed. That documented decision trail is itself evidence that the process worked — not despite the ambiguity, but because I structured my way through it.

**PM Skills This Demonstrates:**
Structured thinking in ambiguous situations · Assumption mapping · Research-driven direction · Comfort with iteration
